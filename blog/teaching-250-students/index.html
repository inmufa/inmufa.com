From 30 students to 250

Some of the best years of my work so far were spent in a classroom, not at a desk. I started as a backend mentor at Astrum Academy in Tashkent, and later taught backend development at Farobiy Academy. Between the two I've worked with well over 250 students.

Astrum: the first 30

At Astrum I looked after a group of 30+ students. I rebuilt the backend curriculum around the basics that hold everything else up: memory in C and C++, Python fundamentals, and Git. More of the group finished the course, by about 20%. I also built small tools and ran live demos so people could see their code do something real, and engagement went up by about 40%.

I did 50+ code reviews there, and I held them to a high standard on purpose. Reading someone else's code carefully, and telling them kindly what to fix, is one of the best ways I know to help a person grow.

Farobiy: the bigger room

At Farobiy Academy I taught Python, Django, FastAPI and PostgreSQL to 250+ students across several cohorts. I designed the curriculum, from building APIs to working with databases to putting a project online. I kept doing code reviews and mentoring, because that's where students stop copying examples and start making their own decisions.

What it gave me

Teaching made me explain things I thought I already understood, and that showed me where I didn't. It also showed me how much a beginner can do with a clear path and someone patient enough to review their work. I carry that into every team I work with now.
